# S5-02 KNC OpenMP chunk size for the force loops (results unchanged)

Sprint 5. Depth: light. No card access.

## Why
Upstream schedules the force loops with `schedule(dynamic, 4096 / sizeof(accels[0]))` = 128 bodies per chunk
(`nbody_grav.c:173,288`; also `nbody_plain.c:372,405`, `nbody_shmem.c:527` guided). At 240 threads only 1, 8 and 157
threads get work for small, medium and large (docs/sprints/sprint-1.md): medium on the card is slower than one laptop
core. Forces per body are independent, so the chunk size cannot change results.

## Do
1. A checked-in patch in `patches/` that makes the chunk a macro (`NBODY_FORCE_CHUNK`, default = upstream's value) in
   those loops; the x86 build keeps the default; the card build sets a KNC value through `config/` or `mk/`. Choose
   the KNC value (fixed or computed from body and thread count) and state the reasoning: enough chunks for 240
   threads on medium (1,024 bodies) without excessive scheduling overhead on large (20,000).
2. Prove results are unchanged on x86: small and medium at chunk sizes 1, 4, 16, 128 and 1, 4, 12 threads give
   likelihood and histogram bit-identical to the references (`scripts/compare.py`). Also large at chunk 4 and 128,
   12 threads.
3. Card build passes `knc_lint`; package still checks (`packaging/check_package.py`); `scripts/card-check.sh`
   still prints the commands.
4. Say what the twin can and cannot tell here (it does not model the OpenMP runtime), and what card run the lead
   should do later (medium-240 and large-240, before vs after).

## Accept
- The x86 comparison output in the Engineer report; patch applied by `scripts/fetch.sh` like the other patches.


## Engineer report

Implemented S5-02 on 2026-11-16. No commits, downloads, toolchain writes or card runs.

`patches/nbody-force-chunk.patch` exposes `NBODY_FORCE_CHUNK` in all five requested
loops. Each translation unit retains its original expression under `#ifndef`,
including `4096 / sizeof(Body)` in the guided loop. Schedule kinds and per-body
arithmetic are unchanged. The existing `scripts/fetch.sh` glob applies it; repeated
fetches succeeded without reapplying it. Dry runs against clean pinned source and
the subsequent wrapper patch in fetch order also passed.

`mk/Makefile` selects 4 for card builds and leaves ordinary x86 at the upstream
default. Four is the largest fixed integer chunk offering at least 240 chunks for
1,024 bodies: 256 instead of eight. Large has 5,000 chunks, about 21 per worker;
four body walks amortize each dispatch, with one quarter as many dispatches as
chunk 1. This is an initial engineering choice, not a measured card optimum.
The guided loop uses 4 as its minimum chunk. Small still offers only 16 chunks.
`FORCE_CHUNK=N` permits experiments; use separate `OUT` directories because make
does not track command-line flag changes. Configuration/patch documentation was added.

scheduling overhead, or full production-duration evolution. Large remains the
bounded 0.05-Gyr workunit. These models exercise tree-force and integration paths;
exact-force and shared-memory paths were not dynamically exercised. The standalone
manifest does not compile `nbody_shmem.c`; its patch is checked structurally.
Concurrent x86 timings are diagnostics, not performance benchmarks.

The twin can inspect generated per-body arithmetic/ISA and estimate isolated
kernel costs under its assumptions. It does not model the OpenMP runtime,
dynamic/guided dispatch, worker contention or OpenMP barrier overhead, so it
cannot establish this change's speedup or choose an optimal chunk. No twin
speedup claim is made.
