## Why
On the large case (20,000 bodies, `workunits/large`), the card takes 30.7 s and the laptop 25.0 s (12 threads), 21.8 s
(vector build) and 136 s (1 thread). The vector force walk gave no card gain, so the force loop is likely not the
dominant term; the serial tree build (`nbMakeTree`, `nbody_tree.c:429`) on one slow card thread is the suspect. We need
measured per-phase times on the card before changing code.

### Tree-build facts (no tree code changed)

All line numbers refer to
`third_party/milkywayathome_client_196/nbody/src/nbody_tree.c`.
There are no OpenMP regions in this file.

- Entry: nbMakeTree, lines 429–459. nbNewTree is called at 435;
  expandBox at 437; insertion at 438–442; centre-of-mass/critical radii
  at 449; threading at 455; optional quadrupoles at 456–457.
- Bounding box: expandBox lines 156–176, serial loop over every body at
  165–170; three absolute-coordinate maxima per body. Root-size doubling
  is serial at 172–175. For finite coordinates, independent per-body maxima
  followed by a maximum produce the same maximum and root size; this is
  not a floating-point sum reduction.
- Insertion: serial body-array loop 438–442 skips zero masses.
  nbLoadBody lines 242–281 descends/modifies shared Subp pointers at
  254–278, allocates cells at 270, and updates maxDepth at 279.
  nbMakeCell lines 179–197 mutates the free list and cellUsed.
  Keeping the exact insertion/allocation order retains the current tree;
  an ordinary parallel-for over this loop does not retain that order.
- Centre of mass: hackCofM lines 386–424 is a serial recursive cell
  traversal, not another flat loop over all bodies. Child indices are
  processed in increasing order at 395–408, recursing at 401 before
  Mass(p) and weighted-position additions at 404–406; division is 410–413.
  Independent child subtrees can finish in parallel before each parent's
  additions, retaining each cell's original child-index addition order.
  A reordered/reduced sum would not retain the specified arithmetic order.
- Critical radius: per-cell findRCrit lines 308–343, called at 422 inside
  hackCofM after its centre of mass is known. It reads the geometric
  Pos(p), cmpos, size and context; Pos(p) is overwritten by cmpos at 423.
  Different cells' radius arithmetic is independent once those inputs are
  available, provided each geometric position is retained until its radius
  calculation. There is no separate critical-radius body-array pass.
- Shared diagnostics: nbCheckTreeStructure lines 373–380 calls
  nbCheckTreeDim 345–370, which mutates tree.structureError and prints the
  first error. Parallel subtree calculations require ordered/serialized
  handling of these diagnostics to retain that behavior.
- Reclaim: nbNewTree lines 200–222 serially scans the old threaded tree
  at 204–216 and pushes cells onto one shared free list at 208–209.
  It is not a flat body-array pass; free-list order is shared state.
- Threading: threadTree lines 127–150 assigns each node's Next and each
  cell's More, enumerating child pointers before recursion at 147.
  With child lists and successor arguments fixed, separate subtree writes
  are disjoint and can be performed in parallel with identical links.
- Quadrupoles: hackQuad lines 70–121 saves child pointers at 80–86 before
  the Subp/Quad union is overwritten, then recursively processes cells at
  93 and accumulates in child order at 88–120. Independent subtrees can
  finish first in parallel; retaining the child-order parent accumulation
  and union lifetime retains the current sums.
